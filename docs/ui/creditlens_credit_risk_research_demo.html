RESEARCH DEMO · CREDIT RISK

Does a credit model work for people with no credit history?

Lenders score applicants from their past borrowing. Applicants with little or none, called thin-file, are the hardest to score. This project trains a default-prediction model, then tests how well it does for them and how well it explains itself.

What you can do here

How it works

1

Train

A gradient-boosted model learns from past loan applications where the repayment outcome is known.

2

Segment

Applicants are split into thin-file and thick-file by bureau history. The rule is fixed before looking at outcomes.

3

Explain

Every score comes with the inputs that pushed it up or down, calculated with SHAP.

4

Check

Accuracy, calibration and error rates are reported per group, including gender and age bands.

How it will be evaluated

  • Thin-file rule: [DEFINITION: set from bureau coverage before looking at outcomes]
  • Main metric: ROC-AUC, to be confirmed against the competition page. Also reported: PR-AUC, KS, Brier score and calibration.
  • Small groups: 95% bootstrap intervals. If the thin-file group is too small for a firm answer, the report says "inconclusive" and does not redefine the group to get a result.
  • Fairness: error rates and calibration compared across gender and age bands. Gaps are reported as findings; no mitigation is claimed.

Limits you should know about

  • The model is trained on Home Credit applications from a Kaggle competition. It does not describe Indian borrowers.
  • The data is used under Kaggle's competition rules, so this project ships no data and no trained model file. You train it yourself from your own download.
  • The data may not include application dates, so evaluation uses random splits, not a test that moves forward in time.
  • The thin-file group may be too small for firm conclusions.
  • Risk bands and cutoffs are illustrative assumptions, not lender policy.
  • This is a research and portfolio project. It must not be used to approve or reject a real applicant.
